I'm looking for my next full-time role in design leadership, and while I look I take on freelance and consulting work. If I take a full-time role partway through an engagement, I will finish the work I agreed to do. Either kind of work starts with an email telling me about what you're chewing on.

## A full-time role

I'm looking for a Director of Product Design role, or better, at a connected devices company of 200 or more people that's close to its customers (or trying to get closer). Device companies need the hardware, the app, and the service to work as one product, for several kinds of users, often in rough settings where a light or a sound has to do a screen's job. I've led the design teams that made that happen for insulin pumps and glucose monitors at Medtronic MiniMed and for routers, smart-home devices, and their apps at Belkin, Linksys, and Wemo. At Belkin I led a 25+ person org across industrial design, UX, and mechanical engineering. Next I'd like to do it for devices in homes, factories, fields, and hospitals: industrial equipment, robots, and medical devices.

AI tools have blurred the lines between product, design, and engineering. A product manager can build a working prototype in an afternoon, and an engineer can generate UI variations in minutes, so any of them can move fast and still miss the problem they set out to solve. Someone has to keep the team focused on what customers need while everyone builds, and that's the part of leadership I care most about. I do it by getting everyone close to customers where they use the product, by trying ideas as working prototypes (the app in the real codebase, plus the lights and buttons and sounds the hardware will have), by letting what customers do decide which ideas get built, and by developing designers with the range to work this way. I wrote more about this in [Designers who ship](/2026/06/03/designers-who-ship/).

I'm in Eugene, Oregon. I can work remotely, in person nearby, or hybrid with occasional office days in Corvallis, Salem, or Portland, and I'm glad to travel. [My resume](https://misc.jonplummer.com/Jon-Plummer-resume.pdf) has the details, and [/about](/about/) has the longer story.

## A limited engagement

I take on engagements with companies that make hardware and software together, with software teams building AI into their products, and with the product development firms that design for both. Most start at one of three moments: a team is about to commit to a new product, customers are struggling with something already shipped, or a company has designers and no design leader.

Every engagement has a price we agree on before work starts: a fixed fee for projects and a monthly fee for advising. If the scope changes, we agree on the change and its price before I do the extra work. The prices below are where each kind of work starts, and the final number depends on how many people, customers, and weeks it involves.

### Concept sprint

A concept sprint takes a blended team – product, design, engineering, architecture, and anyone else who knows the problem well – through one in-person week, from a broad opportunity to a single concept that customers have tried and executives have heard pitched, with a rough cost and timeline for building the first version. I plan the week with you, line up the customer sessions, facilitate, and help the team write up where they landed. I ran Invoca's first concept sprint, and the concept it produced shipped to customers as the AI Messaging Agent. [The Concept Sprint](/2026/05/04/concept-sprint/) describes how a typical week is arranged. A concept sprint starts at $12,000.

### Discovery study

A discovery study finds out what's worth building before anyone builds it. I interview eight to 10 of your customers across two or three types, plus the people at your company who sell, support, and build the product, and bring back which problems are common or painful enough to solve, who has them, and which benefit to go after first. You get a short report, customer behavioral profiles, and a readout with your team in four or five weeks. A discovery study starts at $7,500.

### Design research

Once there's a product or a few designs to react to, research tells you whether it works and which direction to take. I run usability tests, concept tests that compare possible solutions, and focused studies of how people use what you've already shipped, then tell your team what to change and why. Research runs through most of these engagements; this one is for when research is the whole job. At Belkin, my in-home studies and usability tests of [router setup](/portfolio/belkin-router-out-of-box/) drove the changes that, over the run of those products, cut router support calls by roughly 70%. Design research starts at $6,000.

### Design and prototyping

I still do the design work myself. I'll take one flow end to end – setup, alerts, onboarding, wherever people get stuck – and prototype it so customers can try it and engineers can build from it. Much of my work has been where software meets hardware, at Belkin on [Wemo](/portfolio/wemo-smart-home-accessories-and-app/), [Linksys](/portfolio/linksys-app-revitalization/), and [Velop](/portfolio/velop-whole-home-wifi-system/), and before that at Medtronic MiniMed, and lately I've been prototyping with AI tools ([coordinated experience](/portfolio/coordinated-experience-prototype/), [enhanced data dictionary](/portfolio/data-dictionary-prototype/)). Design and prototyping starts at $7,000 for one flow, about a week of work; I'll quote a fixed fee once we agree on the brief.

### Discovery interview training

If you'd rather your own product managers and designers run discovery, I'll teach them how. At Cayuse I ran three sessions over four weeks for product managers, product owners, tech leads, and designers, with homework that had everyone interview at least one customer, and customer contact went up across the team. The training starts with the research question, because it decides more about how the interviews go than anything else. This version adds a session on where the findings go once the interviews are done, since a team that learns something needs a point in its process where a finding can change the plan. [Teaching a product team to do discovery](/2026/10/28/teaching-a-product-team-to-do-discovery/) tells the whole story. Discovery interview training starts at $5,000.

### Advising

Some teams have designers and no design leader, or a product to ship and no designers yet. For them I can cover part of what a head of design does, about four hours a week: reviewing work, running critiques, and contributing to roadmap decisions. The [product trio framework](/portfolio/product-trio/) I wrote to get product, design, and engineering planning together is an example of the kind of change advising can start. We start with one quarter and then decide together whether to keep going. Advising starts at $3,000 a month.

## How an engagement works

We start by talking through what you're trying to do. Sometimes one call covers it, and sometimes it takes a few conversations, a look at the product, or a visit to see it in use before I can scope the work well. Then I send a proposal that names the scope, what's out of scope, the price, and the dates. On a shorter engagement, half the fee is due when we start and half when I deliver; a longer one gets progress payments tied to milestones, set out in the proposal. The final work is yours once it's paid for.

When the work takes me away from Eugene, travel is billed on top of the fee: airfare and lodging at cost, plus a daily per diem at the federal rate for the destination. If I drive, mileage is billed at the IRS rate, plus driving time by the hour.
